In this essay I will explain the main causes of the First World War. The system of alliances divided Europe into two armed camps, the Triple Entente and the Triple Alliance, so a small conflict could quickly involve many countries. Militarism led the great powers to build huge armies and navies, and Germany's naval race with Britain increased the tension. Imperial rivalry over colonies in Africa and Asia created suspicion and competition between the European empires. Nationalism was especially strong in the Balkans, where Slavic groups wanted freedom from Austria-Hungary. The assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand in Sarajevo on 28 June 1914 was the immediate trigger for the war. Austria-Hungary blamed Serbia, and when Russia, Germany, France and Britain honoured their alliances the crisis became a world war. In conclusion, the First World War had many causes and they were all connected.
