In this essay I will explain the main causes of the First World War. The system of alliances split Europe into two armed camps, the Triple Entente and the Triple Alliance, so a minor conflict could rapidly involve many countries. Militarism caused the great powers to build enormous armies and navies, and Germany's naval race with Britain raised the tension. Imperial rivalry over colonies in Africa and Asia created suspicion and competition between the European empires. Nationalism was particularly strong in the Balkans, where Slavic groups wanted independence from Austria-Hungary. The assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand in Sarajevo on 28 June 1914 was the immediate trigger for the war. Austria-Hungary blamed Serbia, and when Russia, Germany, France and Britain honoured their alliances the crisis turned into a world war. In conclusion, the First World War had many causes and they were all connected.
