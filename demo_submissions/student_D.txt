This essay looks at why a murder in the Balkans turned into a world war. Germany, Austria-Hungary and Italy were bound together by treaties, while France, Russia and Britain had their own agreements, so a quarrel between two countries could spread. Several nations were investing heavily in weapons and warships in the years before 1914. Competition for overseas territory also made European governments distrust each other. Serbian nationalists in the Balkans resented Austrian control of Bosnia. On 28 June 1914 a young Bosnian Serb named Gavrilo Princip shot the heir to the Austrian throne in Sarajevo. Within weeks the major powers had declared war on each other because of their promises to defend allies. Overall, no single factor caused the war on its own.
