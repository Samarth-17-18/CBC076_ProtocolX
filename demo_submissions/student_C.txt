In this essay I will explain the main causes of the First World War. Europe was split into two heavily armed blocs, the Triple Entente and the Triple Alliance, which meant a minor dispute could draw in numerous nations. The major powers expanded their armies and fleets, and the naval competition between Germany and Britain made relations even more strained. Competition for colonies in Africa and Asia bred mistrust among the European empires. In the Balkans nationalist feeling ran high as Slavic peoples sought liberation from Austro-Hungarian rule. What actually set the war off was the murder of Archduke Franz Ferdinand at Sarajevo on 28 June 1914. Austria-Hungary held Serbia responsible, and once the allies of each side stepped in, with Russia, Germany, France and Britain all joining, the crisis grew into a global war. In conclusion, the First World War had many causes and they were all connected.
